# NESFIC 2026: Uniform Solution Proposal Requirements (official text)

Copied word for word on 2026-10-30 from `https://startup.assam.gov.in/nesfic26/docs/uspr.pdf`
(one page, the same for every challenge). The 12 portal boxes follow these headings. Our
answers are in [NESFIC_D07_FINAL_ANSWERS.md](NESFIC_D07_FINAL_ANSWERS.md).

> The following proposal structure applies uniformly to every challenge. Applicants should
> provide enough detail for the proposal to be assessed on problem fit, solution quality,
> technical feasibility, development readiness, innovation and team capability. Keep the
> proposal focused on solution development; pilot validation is addressed only after the
> development plan.

| Proposal component | What the applicant should provide |
|---|---|
| 1. Problem understanding & user workflow | Explain the problem, users/beneficiaries and current workflow. Identify the specific gap(s) addressed and the intended outcome. |
| 2. Proposed solution & key features | Describe the solution, main modules/functions, user interaction and what changes versus the current approach. |
| 3. User journey, wireframes & prototype | Provide user-flow/process diagrams and key screen wireframes/mock-ups. Show prototype/demo maturity where available. |
| 4. Technology & solution architecture | Describe stack and architecture: software/hardware, AI/ML models or algorithms, methods/rules, APIs/integrations, data flow and hosting. |
| 5. Development methodology & intermediate steps | Show the sequence from discovery/requirements through design, prototype, MVP, testing and refinement, with key outputs. |
| 6. Development timeline & milestones | Provide an indicative development timeline with phases, milestones, deliverables and dependencies, including when the solution will be ready for validation. |
| 7. Data & information requirements | Specify data/information needed for development and testing, formats, processing/annotation needs, dependencies and assumptions. Do not assume unconfirmed access. |
| 8. Team, manpower & expertise | Identify roles, relevant domain/technical expertise and indicative manpower by development phase. Highlight critical specialist skills. |
| 9. Testing, validation & evidence | Explain development testing, proposed performance/quality measures and evidence of progress. Include relevant prior research, pilots, deployments or product evidence. |
| 10. Security, privacy, safety & compliance | Identify relevant security, privacy, safety, regulatory, interoperability, auditability and human-review requirements and how they will be addressed. |
| 11. Innovation & differentiation | Explain the novel element, relevant existing alternatives/prior art and the basis of differentiation. Mention relevant IP and key open-source/proprietary dependencies. |
| 12. Resources, risks & pilot validation | Provide indicative development/validation resource and cost drivers, key risks and mitigations. Briefly state how the developed solution would subsequently be pilot-validated in the stated context. |

**Assessment dimensions:** problem relevance and understanding; solution completeness and
user fit; technical soundness; feasibility and development plan; innovation/differentiation;
data, security and compliance; team/domain capability; validation evidence; resource
realism; and pilot-validation readiness.
