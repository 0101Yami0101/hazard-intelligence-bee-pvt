APSAC Visit Prep

Arunachal Pradesh Space Application Centre, ItanagarPrepared 22–23 Oct 2026Scope: whole state

We build statewide landslide and flood forecasts for Arunachal on a shared data platform, and APSAC's portal already feeds that model. From this visit we want three things: statewide data at finer scale than what's openly served, landslide records with dates, and a written OK to use their layers. In return we offer a daily, self-checking forecast layer on top of their hazard maps.

01Who APSAC is, and who sits around it

APSAC (formerly SRSAC) sits under the Department of Science & Technology and is the state's nodal agency for remote sensing, GIS and GPS. A notification of 29 Feb 2016 (No. APSAC/18/2016-17) makes it the nodal agency for every government department and PSU. It runs APSSDI, the state spatial data portal our geology comes from. Its site gives 1995 as the founding year on one page and 1996 on another.

How much, in short: 9 of the 24 open APSSDI layers are on disk (76 MB). Three reach the product: lithology, lineaments and settlements. In the landslide model, the two geology inputs carry 13.8% of the total input weight, but removing them costs only 0.004 AUC. Their settlements are the app's place search. The flood model uses nothing from APSSDI. The state outline and districts come from GADM, not APSSDI.

The portal's full catalogue (public dataset list, read 23 Oct): 113 datasets, every one marked "Free". They are the 24 layers above plus 89 "Aerial imagery" tiles covering the Itanagar capital region (the 90-cell aerial survey grid). Signed in, the dashboard counts 140 datasets (about 92 raster, 48 vector) and 171 documents. So about 27 datasets and all the documents are visible only with an account. We have not seen what they are.

The APSSDI portal publishes no licence, though its catalogue tags every dataset "Free". The app credits APSSDI on the landslide method page and in the Data Backbone. Our own code notes say to confirm attribution with APSAC before their data goes into paid work.

Why the high country needs their field data most: 42% of the land we model sits at 2,000–4,000 m. There the model gives High+ to 10.5% of land, but that band holds 20.3% of known slides. The likely cause is that fewer slides get mapped where access is hard. Field-checked high-altitude slides from SILAAS go straight at that gap.

05Likely questions, with checked answers

Their data and ours

QuestionAnswer
What of ours do you use?APSSDI lithology and lineaments as model inputs; settlements for place search; SILAAS 2023 and the 2014/2017 Bhuvan inventories (11,329 slides) as training answers. Everything came from openly served map services; nothing behind a login was bypassed
How, without an account?APSSDI's map server (GeoServer at apssdi.in) answers without a login, while the catalogue page needs one. Fetched 28 Jul, credited in the app
Did you ask permission?Not yet; it's one of our asks today. We don't redistribute their raw files. Their data shapes the model and the place search
Why not use our hazard maps as an input?A model trained on another map learns that map, not landslides. Official maps stay benchmarks, as GSI's does
Your map vs ours?We haven't seen theirs. Against GSI's national map on the same cells: 85 vs 60 in 100; on slides GSI never saw, 86 vs 58. A state model beating a national 3-class map is the expected result
What area and resolution?The whole state on a 100 m grid (8.2 million cells). Terrain from the 30 m Copernicus DEM, slopes worked out at 25 m. Rain at 11 km (IMERG history) and about 22 km (live forecast). 14.3% of the state (flat land, ice, water) is shown as not assessed, never as low risk
Which satellite data?IMERG rain (26 years daily), Sentinel-1 radar, Copernicus DEM, ESA WorldCover, SoilGrids, ERA5-Land soil wetness, GloFAS. No Indian imagery yet: LISS-IV and Cartosat would be new
NESDR / Bhuvan?Bhuvan: landslide inventories used; the 2003–2020 flood layer was tested and rejected. NESDR: land cover found, not used. NESAC says that layer was never checked on the ground (80–85% self-scored)

The model

QuestionAnswer
Method?WHERE: gradient-boosted trees (LightGBM) on 34 ground facts, trained on 37,788 mapped slides. WHEN: how unusual the last 3 and 7 days of rain are at that exact spot. Forecast = weak ground × unusual rain
Why not deep learning?What we're short of is dates, not locations. Trees on tabular facts beat deep nets at this size, train in minutes, and every score can be explained input by input
How accurate?Location: AUC 0.859 ± 0.016 on held-out regions, about 6 times in 7. Timing: 0.768 on 72 dated events. Places and days at High have had about 3× the usual landslides, Very High about 10× (84 dated slides, rough)
Is it a probability?Landslide: no, a rank and a "times the usual" figure. Flood at a gauged river: yes, the chance of reaching the danger line
Does it just find roads?No. Terrain and drainage alone (10 inputs) give 0.839, 97.7% of the full model
Flood method?WHERE trained on 2,185 verified floods (radar, Global Flood Database, gauges). WHEN: rain here and upstream, soil wetness on flat ground, river levels at 32 gauges. A 2-day level line at CWC gauges
Flood accuracy?Timing: AUC 0.879 on 44 unseen Arunachal floods, 2023–25. On past years, a forecast of 20% at a gauged river came true about 20% of the time. In the live log, no river has reached its line yet, so it is unproven on a live flood

Scope, us and the arrangement

QuestionAnswer
Is this for one district?No, it's statewide by design: every input, model and forecast covers the whole state. District and township requests are outputs drawn from it. One such request, from Jang township, is already in hand

06Hard questions and weak spots

Our weakest points statewide are the high country, the gauge feeds, and having no live catch yet.
