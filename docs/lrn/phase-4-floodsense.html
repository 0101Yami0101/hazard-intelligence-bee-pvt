FloodSense, the flood forecast

1Three numbers, never mixed

FloodSense answers three different questions. Each has its own badge on screen. Mixing them up is the most common mistake, so learn these first.

Forecast ✔Will this river reach its line?
  • At 32 river gauges, 1–9 days ahead
  • Reads the river itself + rain
  • Calibrated: when it says 20%, about 20% happen
  • Graded every day against the gauge
Outlook ≈A flood within ~10 km this week?
  • For every place in the state
  • Ranks well (about 90 in 100)
  • Approximate: failed its calibration test; shown by the user's decision with its error
Estimate ◆If a flood comes, does water reach this spot?
  • Each 100 m square, from terrain shape
  • The same every day
  • Shown capped at "20%+", with its checked rate
Forecast ✔Outlook ≈Estimate ◆Measured ●Input ☂Record ◷the six badges on screen
🚫 Never say "this spot has X% chance of flooding today". Spot × day is tiny, can't be checked, and runs ~4× high inside Arunachal. The river forecast is the strongest number. Lead with it.

🔗 Same trick as SlideSense: every input is "how unusual is this for here". Rain is compared with each point's own history. A river's level is measured as its share of the way from its ordinary level (0) to its line (1). That lets a 5 m trunk river and a 0.5 m hill stream share one model.

3The flood record: what "a flood" means here

Every model learns from past floods. This is the story of how the list of past floods was fixed, the most important fix in the module.

Each verified flood keeps the list of routes that confirm it. Routes overlap, so they add up to more than 2,180.

🚨 The radar check that passed everything

100%

The early radar screen "confirmed" flood reports, but when tested on known dry dates at the same places, it passed those too. It had only ever been checked on 4 real floods. So the old list was really just news reports.

✅ The new radar test, written down before running
dry dates correctly "no"
97.8%

It compares a flood-date image with the same place in the dry season (up to 15 passes) and the same season, looking only at low, flat, normally-dry ground. New water must drop more than 3 dB to below −18 dB. It failed narrowly on attempt 1, was fixed, and passed on fresh records. A radar "no" doesn't disprove a report: floods can drain before the satellite passes.

Verified floods by year (all routes; the region the models train on, not only Arunachal)

Thin before 2014: those are old news reports that Google's Global Flood Database confirmed. Regular radar coverage starts in 2015. The record ends in Oct 2025, because confirmations arrive late.

4WHERE: if a flood comes, does water reach this square?

A tree model (LightGBM) on 15 terrain-shape facts only, learned from 2,806 satellite-confirmed floods, then calibrated into a real chance. Read it as "given a flood in this catchment", not a yearly chance.

What it leans on share of the model's use

HAND (height above the nearest stream, Phase 1) leads, then the size of that river. Valley shape within 2 km tells an open plain from a gorge.

Physical check: chance by height above the stream

Falls to zero by 25 m above the stream, as physics says it must. The top 0.1% of squares statewide sit at a median 0 m above the channel, 0.4° slope, 136 m elevation: real floodplain.

The same model with land cover scored higher, and put its top squares on glacial lakes 360 m above any river.
🏔️ The out-of-distribution trap, in one line: 97% of training squares are below 500 m, so no test block ever asked about a mountain. "Permanent water" means a river channel at 200 m and a glacial lake at 3,000 m. A better test score is not proof. The fix is physical checks the tests can't fake (check_flood_where_sanity.py), and using causes (shape) rather than place-names (land cover, height).

5The river forecast: the strongest number

At each of 32 gauges: the chance the river reaches its line, 1 to 9 days ahead. It reads the river's last reading, its last week, and the rain.

Results are also reported on days when the river was still below its line, the warning people actually need. A river already above is easy to "predict".
Two kinds of line, never blur them. 7 gauges use CWC's official danger level: "chance it crosses danger". The others use the river's own wettest 2% of days: real, but a home-made line. Doimukh and Tamen moved to their own 2%: they had never truly reached danger.
32 gauges, two networks. 18 CWC (big and medium rivers, levels in metres, live) and 14 NWDP (small rivers, flow, 2–3 days late). Today (2 Nov) the whole NWDP feed is down, so those 14 fall back to rain only and the screen says so.

7One wrong reading = a "52% flood"

On 28 Sep, CWC's feed held one reading of 2,231.30 m at Murga Bridge against a steady 2,134.30 m. Probably another station's value under the wrong code. The forecast read 52%.

Why the old check missed it: it flagged a reading 3× its neighbours. On heights above sea level (2,134 m) that never fires.

Now, three passes: (1) the old ratio test for zeros and slipped decimals; (2) impossible excursions: a level that leaves a steady stretch by more than 5 m and comes straight back within 72 h; (3) keying slips: a swapped digit (210.5 for 201.5).

On all 1.18 M readings: 194 removed, 18 false high-water days, all 18 confirmed false by four separate tests. The real landslide-dam surge at Tuting (Oct 2018) kept all 144 of its readings.

🧬 What the false readings had done: Doimukh and Tamen had never really reached danger. And gauge-found "floods" resting on false readings were in the training list. Five are now dropped by rule. Removing a handful of no-rain "floods" lifted timing in Arunachal from 0.879 to 0.895: false floods on dry days teach a model that floods come from nothing.

8The high-river fault: smaller, still open

A river already above its line can be told about 0% for the next 1–4 days, though many such days stay above. Say this before anyone finds it.

Adopted by the user's decision knowing it failed this one test, because it is far better everywhere else. Most failing days: Rho Basti, June 2016, a melt-fed river sitting 0–21 cm above its line, where "flood" hangs on centimetres.

Seven designs tried, each with rules written first
  1. Floor (high never below low): failed
  2. S-curve for high-river days: better, not cured
  3. Each river's "habit" of staying high: failed. It's spells, not habits
  4. The river's own week as inputs: the big gain, now live
  5. Upstream gauges + melt + level line: not cured
  6. "At its line" flag: covered 79–91% of days, useless
  7. Fresh melt at high gauges: failed year to year

Decided: no eighth design on this history. More tries on the same few hundred days would find luck, not a cure. What helps is new information: more monsoons logged, gauges further upstream, dam schedules.

9"Near here": the outlook for every place

Where there's no gauge, people still ask: "will it flood near me?" This is that number, and it's the approximate one.

How it's made
  1. Day model, per ~10 km rain area: how unusual today's rain is here and upstream × how much floodable ground the area has (from terrain, not from where floods were reported, so a valley nobody writes news about isn't told it never floods). Flat areas also read soil wetness.
  2. Combine the areas a 10 km circle touches, then the 7 days.
  3. Map to how often a recorded flood really fell within 10 km (calibration).

"× more" = recorded floods compared with what it said.

⚖️ Why "approximate": it failed its pre-registered test (Gate A). The flood record's own rate doubled in 2024–25 compared with the rain, so no fixed level holds from year to year. It ranks well, so by the user's decision (27 Sep) it ships labelled approximate, with its measured error on screen, and is logged daily. And it counts recorded floods: unreported ones aren't in the truth, so it's a floor.

🧭 The decision (the user's, 19 Aug and 6 Oct): the tech must be our own, proven on Arunachal. Google's open-sourced model (June 2026) may sit beside ours as a second opinion, credited, never as the core. Its real advantage is scale: ~16,000 rivers against our 32. That is the honest gap.

13The daily run and the track record

Every morning at 08:30 IST in the cloud (Modal, Mumbai region, since 20 Oct): fetch rain, soil, gauges → clean → forecast → log → grade → publish a small snapshot the app reads.

📒 Read it honestly: logging began as the monsoon ended, so no river has crossed its line yet. "Too few to judge" is the true status. The 3 warnings, 0 right are the 29 Sep false reading at Murga Bridge, kept in the log on purpose. Nothing is rewritten after the fact.

14The limits card

🌧️Hasn't been through a live flood yet. Logging began as the 2026 monsoon ended. 2027 is the real test.
≈Near-here is approximate: the record's rate moved ~2× between years. Ranks well; the level isn't stable.
🏞️High-river fault: smaller, still open; needs new information, not another design.
📡Feeds go down. NWDP is offline today, and some CWC gauges have been for months. Falls back to rain only and says so.
⛰️Flash floods in gorges are hard: WHERE ranks steep ground at only 0.67.
📰Truth = recorded floods. Unreported floods aren't counted, so outlooks are floors.
📏No depth or extent map: needs river cross-sections or LiDAR (Ask / Paid tier).
🔢32 gauges vs Google's ~16,000: the real data gap.
