1The whole idea in one picture

An app is a vehicle. The backbone is the road. We build the road, then run vehicles on it.

💡 Lead with the road, not a vehicle. We are not "a landslide app". The forecasts are the proof that the road carries weight.

3The problem: four walls

Every hazard team hits the same four walls. The backbone knocks down all four, once.

🧩
Data in piecesHeight every 30 m, soil every 250 m, rain every 11 km, rock as outlines, rivers as lines. A dozen agencies.
🔁
Every project starts from zeroMonths lining data up, one map published, the work thrown away.
🥀
Maps go staleGround barely changes. Rain and rivers change every day.
📋
Nobody keeps scoreForecasts are never written down and checked. So nothing learns.
🔑 How we learned this: building the first landslide forecast, the model was the small part. Finding, checking and lining up the data was most of the work, and all of it could be reused.

5One square of ground

The state is cut into 8.2 million squares of 100 m × 100 m, each about the size of a football field. Every source becomes one more layer over the same squares. Pick a square and read straight down.

👆 hover or tap a layer

Each square gets 34 facts

…and every place gets 26 years of daily rain. Together that table is "shelf one". It is the same for every product.
◭ SlideSense◈ FloodSense◬ SoilSense
solid = uses it today · dashed = planned · grey = not used
🧱 One file, three products. The ground-height file comes in as 28 satellite tiles. The backbone turns it into steepness, slope facing, drainage and height above the river. SlideSense reads the steepness. FloodSense reads the height above the river. SoilSense reads the slope again.

6The six rules (plus three the pitch adds)

These rules are why anyone should trust what comes out. Learn each with its "why".

1
One map for everythinga model needs a table, and two products can never disagree about where a place is
2
Keep the original, record where it came fromanyone can audit a number. If a source dies we lose freshness, not the system
3
Check a source before trusting itofficial data can be wrong: the official flood map put most "flooded" ground on steep slopes
4
The backbone doesn't know the hazardnothing in it says "landslide" or "flood", which is what makes the next product cheap
5
Two shelvesshelf 1 is ready-to-use data (a road planner wants this). Shelf 2 is the answers (an officer wants this)
6
Close the loopmore data makes a forecast fresher. Only knowing what happened makes it better
🧪 Test on unseen ground. A model is judged only on places it never saw during building.
🏷️ Label every number. Each number on screen says what it is: a forecast, a measurement or a record.
🤚 Say so when unsure. Where data can't support an answer, the app says so and doesn't guess.

7Every hazard is WHERE × WHEN

This one equation holds the whole project together. A product is just this question asked of the backbone.

WHEREcan it happen here?
the ground · barely changes
×
WHENis something setting it off?
rain, river · changes daily
=
FORECASTwhat to watch,
for the next 7 days
🔫 ✗ ✋ ✗
unloaded, no trigger
nothing
🔫 ✓ ✋ ✗
loaded, no trigger
nothing today
🔫 ✗ ✋ ✓
unloaded, trigger
nothing
🔫 ✓ ✋ ✓
loaded + trigger
landslide

🔫 The loaded gun. A weak slope is a loaded gun (WHERE). Heavy rain pulls the trigger (WHEN). You need both. That's why a "risk map" alone is not the product: it says nothing about today.

🗣️ Vocabulary: scientists call WHERE "susceptibility" and WHEN "trigger". To clients we only say "Landslide Forecast" and "Flood Forecast". Susceptibility is an ingredient, not the meal.

10Don't say → say instead

Each wrong phrase on the left has been tempting before. Experts catch every one.

✗ don't say
✓ say instead
"We are a landslide app."
"We build the data backbone. The forecasts are the proof."
"It is 86% accurate."
"Landslide WHERE picks the right spot about 6 times in 7, on ground it never saw."
"High means a 70% chance of a landslide."
"A landslide level is a rank: High means look here first. High has seen about 3× the usual landslides."
"We caught a real flood."
"Not yet. It was tested hard on past years. The next monsoon is the proof."
"The loop is closed."
"Closed for floods. Landslides are written down daily but not graded yet."
"We own unique data."
"Most inputs are public. Ours is the assembly, the upkeep and the checked history."
"It works for any state."
"It moved to Assam in about four weeks. Each new state needs its own records and checks."
"Others build on our feed. We send SMS alerts."
"Planned." (Some pitch slides show planned things. Say so.)
