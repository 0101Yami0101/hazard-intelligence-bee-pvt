1Who they are

APSAC (Arunachal Pradesh Space Application Centre, formerly SRSAC) is the state's nodal agency for remote sensing, GIS and GPS for every department (notification of 29 Feb 2016), under the state's Department of Science & Technology.

NRSC (ISRO)Runs Bhuvan. Partner on SILAAS (landslides) and SISDP-U (1:10K maps).
SAC (ISRO)Runs VEDAS. Partner on NWIA (wetlands and glacial lakes).
IIRS, DehradunISRO's training institute.
APSACItanagar · ~20 staff, 10+ scientists
APSSDI: the state spatial data portal APSAC runs. Its map server is open; its catalogue needs a login.
Data centre: 6 servers, 38 TB · LiDAR processing (no scanner) · camera drones, 6 drone ports
NESAC (Umiam)Runs NESDR. Runs NE flood early warning (operational in Assam; Arunachal unknown) and lightning nowcasting.
DST, Govt of AP · NECParent department (sanction route) · funder of most NE space projects.
APSDMA / DDMAsA separate department: who acts on a warning.

2The list: every item, once

Tap a box to filter. Every item carries three kinds of label, explained below. Tick an item when it's been asked for or pulled; ticks are saved.

Who it helpsSlideSense FloodSense SoilSense (if it moves to Arunachal) future module platform
Always ask statewide. The biggest gap is dates: WHERE learns from 37,788 mapped slides, but WHEN is tested on only 72 dated events.
📊 In numbers: 9 of APSSDI's 24 open layers are on our disk (76 MB); 3 reach the product (rock type, faults, villages). The Bhuvan/SILAAS inventories give 11,329 of our 37,788 training slides (30%). The flood model uses nothing from APSSDI; the state outline and districts come from GADM.
🔓 How we got what we use: APSSDI's catalogue needs a login, but its map server (apssdi.in/geoserver/ows) answers openly. Fetched 28 Jul 2026 and credited in the app; nothing behind a login was bypassed. APSSDI publishes no licence, so written permission is a HIGH ask.
⚖️ How much we depend on their geology: rock type and fault lines carry 13.8% of the landslide model's use (by SHAP), but terrain carries much the same signal: dropping rock type costs only 0.004 AUC (0.859 → 0.855).

3What we offer back

Their maps answer where. Ours adds when, every day, on the same ground.

live
A daily "when" layer, statewide
7-day landslide outlook for any place or road; flood chances at 32 gauged rivers, plus an area outlook.
live
A public score sheet
Every river forecast written down before the day and checked against the gauge.
ready
An independent score of their maps
The same test we ran on GSI's map: ranking, capture at equal area, where maps disagree.
built as files
The Data Backbone as APSSDI layers
29 sources on one 100 m grid, served the way APSSDI already serves layers.
planned
Field data in their SILAAS format
Any field visit logs slides and flood marks in FLIM-compatible fields.
joint product
Hazard on their route planning
They plan road and power-line routes; we add landslide hazard to each option. Drone surveys go to their team when a client funds them.
🧰 Fixes we found, to hand over politely: settlements miss Itanagar and 5 HQ towns · SILAAS report/map/dataset links return 404 · most project pages in their menu return 404 · the map server dropped offline for ~30 minutes at a time in Jul–Aug. (The certificate fault is already fixed.)
